Galaxies

A little bit about galaxies

Galaxies are a massive system of stars, gas, dust, and dark matter all bound together by gravity. Often described as the "cities" of the universe, galaxies contain anywhere from millions to trillions of stars and vary greatly in shape, size, and composition.

Types of Galaxies:

Astronomers typically classify galaxies into three main shapes:
Spiral: These have a flat, rotating disk with a bright center and spiraling arms. They are often rich in gas and dust, making them active sites for new star birth. Our home, the Milky Way, is a spiral galaxy.
Elliptical: Ranging from round to highly elongated, these galaxies primarily contain older stars and have very little gas or dust.
Irregular: These galaxies have chaotic, unorganized shapes, usually caused by the gravitational pull or collision of other galaxies

Key Features

Gravity: It acts as the cosmic glue, holding all the stars, dark matter, and interstellar clouds in orbit around a common center of mass.
Supermassive Black Holes: Most large galaxies contain a supermassive black hole at their exact center, which helps anchor the entire system.
Scale: Galaxies are enormous, ranging from dwarf galaxies spanning a few hundred light-years to giant galaxies stretching over a million light-years across.

Our Home: The Milky Way

Earth and our entire solar system are located inside the Milky Way galaxy. It is a barred spiral galaxy containing over 100 billion stars and measuring about 100,000 light-years across. When you look up at the night sky and see a dense band of light stretching across the dark, you are looking directly into the disk of the Milky Way.

Outside of our galaxy, there are estimated to be up to 2 trillion other galaxies spread throughout the observable universe.
